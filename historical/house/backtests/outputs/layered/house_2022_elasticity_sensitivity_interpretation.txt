2022 House District Elasticity Sensitivity Interpretation
=========================================================

Decision
--------
Do not add the tested district-level elasticity estimates to the
production House forecasting model.

The historical data infrastructure, estimator, component, and sensitivity
runner should be retained for future research and improved district mapping.

Benchmark
---------
Layer 2 uses:
- 2020 presidential district baseline on 2022 boundaries
- calibrated 2022 national environment
- 2.0-point symmetric incumbency adjustment

Layer 2 performance on 400 ordinary D-vs-R races:
- Mean absolute error: 4.889978
- RMSE: 6.475034
- Winner accuracy: 0.957500
- Brier score: 0.034720
- Log loss: 0.125631
- Predicted Democratic win-count error: 5 seats
- Expected Democratic win-count error: 2.493523 seats

Layer 3 test
------------
District elasticity was estimated from consecutive 2012-2020 House
two-party swings using constrained ordinary least squares through the
origin.

District estimates were partially pooled through a shrinkage grid from
0.00 through 1.00. Each tested elasticity vector was normalized to a
mean of 1.0 among scored races so the experiment isolated district-level
variation from the overall national-environment coefficient.

The 1.00 shrinkage setting reproduced Layer 2 exactly, validating the
experimental control.

Best combined-rank setting
--------------------------
Shrinkage strength: 0.80
District-level variation retained: 0.20

Performance:
- Mean absolute error: 4.892584
- Change from Layer 2 MAE: +0.002606
- RMSE: 6.465780
- Change from Layer 2 RMSE: -0.009255
- Winner accuracy: 0.955000
- Change from Layer 2 winner accuracy: -0.002500
- Brier score: 0.034709
- Change from Layer 2 Brier score: -0.000011
- Log loss: 0.125262
- Change from Layer 2 log loss: -0.000370
- Predicted Democratic win-count error: 4 seats
- Expected Democratic win-count error: 2.354243 seats

Interpretation
--------------
The best partially pooled elasticity setting produced extremely small
improvements in RMSE, Brier score, log loss, and seat-count error, but
slightly worsened mean absolute error and winner accuracy.

More aggressive retention of district-level elasticity variation clearly
degraded performance. With no shrinkage, MAE rose to 5.051948 and RMSE
rose to 6.602405.

The evidence therefore does not support adopting the current elasticity
implementation. The estimated district variation appears to contain
substantial noise from candidate effects, unusual elections, limited
district observations, and the transfer of pre-2022 district-number
estimates across the 2022 redistricting boundary.

Production disposition
----------------------
- Keep Layer 2 as the official historical benchmark.
- Do not add district-level elasticity to the production forecast.
- Preserve all Layer 3 historical datasets, estimators, and sensitivity
  tools.
- Reconsider Layer 3 only after constructing a geographically compatible
  crosswalk or estimating responsiveness from stable demographic and
  geographic district characteristics.
- Do not use the current production model's heuristic elasticity values
  as substitutes for empirically validated historical estimates.

Layer 3 status: RESEARCH COMPLETE; NOT ADOPTED
